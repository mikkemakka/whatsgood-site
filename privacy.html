Privacy

Updated 3 October 2026

What's Good keeps what you write on your iPhone. There's no account and no server, and no one at What's Good can read it.

What the app keeps

What you write (your letter and your good things), the map the app draws from them, and your settings, such as your bedtime reminder. All of it stays on your iPhone.

How your map is made

Apple Intelligence reads what you write and draws your map, on your iPhone. Your words aren't sent to Apple or anyone else to do it.

What isn't collected

No analytics, no tracking and no ads. The app doesn't use the internet, and it doesn't collect any personal information.

Reminders

The bedtime reminder is set up on your iPhone, only if you ask for it. Nothing is sent from a server.

Backups

Like every app's, what you write is part of your iPhone's backup when you back it up to iCloud or a computer, protected the way Apple protects backups.

Testing with TestFlight

While you're testing, Apple shares crash reports and any feedback you choose to send with the developer, as the TestFlight app describes. A screenshot you send with feedback can show what you wrote, so look at it before you send it.

Deleting

Settings › Delete all my data removes everything you've written, and your map, for good. Deleting the app does the same.

If this changes

If What's Good adds iCloud sync, what you write will be kept in your own private iCloud, which only you can read. This page will say so before that happens.

Not a medical service

What's Good is a habit for noticing what's good, not a medical or mental health service.
